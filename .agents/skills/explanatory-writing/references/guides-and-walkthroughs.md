# Guides and source walkthroughs

## Conceptual guide or research explanation

Start with the reader's question and a concrete mental model. Build from a simple case toward the full mechanism, then discuss consequences and relevant trade-offs. For a comparison or decision report, give the main finding early and organize the evidence around the decision. Choose the structure that serves the purpose rather than turning every document into a tutorial.

Separate the observations from the conclusions they support. Explain why a difference matters in the stated context. Include recommendations only when they follow from the evidence and help answer the question.

When teaching a process, include a worked illustrative input, choice, or result appropriate to the question. An outline of a future demonstration can be useful planning material, but should not substitute for the example that explains the process. A guide to choosing a page topic could show one query, a proposed title, and a short opening answer without building a website or claiming measured search performance.

Define an example's conditions once, then follow its reasoning. Keep a complete model or comparison available when useful, while introducing a representative case before a dense table. Retain relevant source distinctions and limits without repeating them at every transition.

Let transitions carry the reasoning forward. A result can raise a question, expose a limit, or make a next step useful. Explain that connection rather than merely announcing the next topic. For example, a count of unfinished plans can lead to checking the records, then investigating the difficulty, then choosing a change. Use the connection that fits the subject; this sequence is not a required outline.

A synthesis should reveal a relationship, implication, or choice that was harder to see in the separate parts. Explain what becomes clearer when the parts are considered together. Shorten or omit a recap that only repeats labels, counts, or definitions without helping the reader use them.

## Walkthrough of real source code

Select small excerpts that expose the behavior being taught. Identify their source and version when reproducibility matters. Mark omitted lines and label simplified examples so the reader can distinguish them from source excerpts.

Trace how an input, value, or decision moves through the code. Explain unfamiliar syntax where it affects the behavior. Follow an excerpt with a small use case and its result when that makes the mechanism clearer. Introduce surrounding framework details only as needed to understand the path being traced.
