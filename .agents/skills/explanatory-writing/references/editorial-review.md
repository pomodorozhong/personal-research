# Editorial review

Read the document in order from the intended reader's starting point. Evaluate explanations and relationships, rather than counting keywords or treating every occurrence of first person, a table, or a qualification as a defect.

Check whether:

- The opening establishes a useful purpose and the depth matches the question.
- A skimming reader can understand who did what, what changed, and why it matters without decoding shorthand.
- Supporting documents explain each example locally and link to the main guide for broader concepts; shared fields help readers compare cases.
- Terms and prerequisites appear before the reader must use them; a taxonomy does not postpone the first understandable example.
- Examples show enough actual illustrative input, choice, or result to teach the mechanism. A proposed outline is identified accurately.
- Tables are readable and serve comparisons or reference; they do not silently carry several unexplained lessons.
- Each section advances the explanation, and observations have an interpretation.
- Topic indexes link to named guides and describe their actual coverage.
- Personal voice reflects supplied views or experiences, and the document makes sense without the original prompt.
- Sources, uncertainty, and material limits remain clear; fictional labels and qualifications are repeated only when useful.
- Research notes describe relevant evidence limits without unnecessary retrieval narration.

For a critique, identify a specific passage, explain the difficulty it creates for this reader, and suggest a targeted rule or revision. Keep useful material and necessary cautions. Do not manufacture a flaw to give every document equal criticism.

Use exact local line or notebook cell references for workspace documents. For PRs, verify the current changed passage and use a link to the relevant file and lines, preferably pinned to the reviewed revision. Place that link immediately after the critique. Do not label reconstructed or hypothetical wording as a quote.

When assessing another skill's coverage, distinguish an explicit rule, a broad rule that needs strengthening, and a missing rule. An instruction's presence shows coverage, not proof that agents will apply it successfully.

For a table-based review, use [the review table template](../assets/review-table-template.md). Fill it with actual evidence and replace all placeholders. If no actionable issues are found, say so instead of producing placeholder critiques. Follow a different presentation when requested.
