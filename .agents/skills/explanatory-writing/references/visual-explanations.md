# Figures alongside explanations

Choose a figure because it helps answer a question about the subject. The prose should explain the behavior, relationship, or consequence it reveals. A diagram of a customer journey supports understanding that journey; routine instructions about reading bands, arrows, or colors can distract from it.

Explain visual conventions when their meaning is unfamiliar or needed to interpret the result accurately. Keep necessary units, scales, observation windows, and distinctions between groups available in labels or a concise caption. A brief note that two panels measure separate outcomes may prevent a false conclusion; a generic chart-reading tutorial usually adds little.

Place a figure where the reader has enough context to understand its question. Introduce the situation before it, then develop the interpretation nearby. Choose the order by the explanation: a whole-process view can establish orientation, or it can later connect details the reader has already learned. Avoid stacking several figures before explaining what each contributes.

When adding a figure to an existing document, reconsider the surrounding prose and transitions. Remove narration that merely repeats its labels, while retaining the reasoning and limitations needed to understand the subject. A recap figure should make a relationship or choice clearer.

Keep figures visually distinct from nearby prose when their boundaries are unclear. A subtle border, background, or spacing can help; choose a treatment suited to the rendering format. The writing guidance does not require a particular chart type, decoration, or interactive tool.
